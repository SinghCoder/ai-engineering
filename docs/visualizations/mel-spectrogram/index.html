Audio features · interactive explainer

From waveform to mel spectrogram

What does one audio feature frame contain? Follow a short window of sound from raw samples to one column of mel-band energies.

1 · Pick a sound and move the window

Make a prediction: If you move the window twice as far at each step, will each frame cover twice as much sound? Change the hop from 10 to 20 ms, then check the window and frame count.

Click a plot or use the frame slider. The shaded region shows the window; the line marks its center.

2 · Compare Hz bins with mel bands

Both plots analyze the same sound at the same times. The upper plot keeps evenly spaced frequency bins. The lower plot combines them into overlapping bands, spaced evenly on the mel scale: narrower in Hz at the low end, wider at the high end.

Relative power: −65 dB 0 dB (peak of each plot)

In these plots, each frame is drawn as a column; model inputs are often written with frames as rows.

3 · Take one mel value apart

Click the mel plot or move the band slider. The triangle shows how much weight the chosen mel band gives to each Hz bin. Multiply each bin’s power by its weight, sum, and then take the log.

Zoomed around the selected band. Solid line: selected frame’s spectrum (relative dB). Dashed triangle: band weights (0 to 1, scaled separately).

Try this

  • Play Hum → hiss → rising tone. Find the hum’s horizontal harmonics, the hiss’s broad patch, and the rising tone’s slope.
  • Switch to Two close tones and compare 10 ms with 60 ms. A longer window can separate nearby frequencies better. Then change just the hop: it changes how often you measure, not what each window covers.
  • With a Short click, try the same windows. The longer one spreads the click across more time in the spectrogram.

Where the model comes in: Each column above is a feature vector, not a word or token ID. An audio model can send that sequence through an audio encoder or, as Mel-LLM demonstrates, use a lightweight front end and feed projected features into the language model without a dedicated speech Transformer.

Calculation notes

Generated mono audio at 16 kHz, 2.4 seconds. Centered Hann windows use zero padding at the ends and a 1024-point FFT. We use unnormalized power and triangular filters spaced by the HTK mel formula. The dB transform uses 10 log₁₀(power/reference); colors clip below −65 dB and each plot has its own reference. The filter triangle is linear in Hz between its three vertices. This is a teaching implementation, not a checkpoint-specific feature extractor.
